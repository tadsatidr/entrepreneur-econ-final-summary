Ch 1–2 · demand and elasticity

Own price moves you along the curve. Income, preference, a higher substitute price, and a lower complement price shift demand right.

Market quantity at one price is the sum of individual quantities. The diagram: at $12, 4 + 5 + 100 = 109. At $11, 5 + 6 + 130 = 141.

\[ E=\frac{\%\Delta Q}{\%\Delta P}=\frac{\Delta Q}{\Delta P}\times\frac{\bar P}{\bar Q} \]

Averages: \(\bar P=(P_1+P_2)/2\), \(\bar Q=(Q_1+Q_2)/2\). Worked point: 10→12 and 100→50 gives \(\bar P=11\), \(\bar Q=75\), 18.2% and −66.7%, size 3.67, elastic.

Elastic > 1. Inelastic < 1. Unit = 1. Perfectly elastic = \(\infty\). Perfectly inelastic = 0. Signed example: \(E=-2\) means a 1% price cut raises sales 2%.

Revenue. Elastic: price up, revenue down; price down, revenue up. Inelastic: the opposite. Unit elastic: revenue unchanged.

Cross-price. Positive = substitutes. Negative = complements.

Income. Positive = normal; inside that, above 1 = luxury, below 1 = necessity. Negative = inferior.

Ch 1–2 · regression

\[ Q^d=\beta_0+\beta_1 P_x+\beta_2 P_z+\beta_3 Y+u \]

Reject \(H_0:\beta=0\) when p < \(\alpha\). \(\alpha\in\{0.01,0.05,0.10\}\).

Stars: p = 0.000 → ***. p = 0.095 → * only. p = 0.57 → no star. p = 0.037 → **.

Significant slope: a one-unit increase changes quantity by \(b\) units, other factors held constant. Insignificant slope: no significant effect, and the elasticity is set to 0.

\[ E_d=b_1\frac{\bar P}{\bar Q},\quad E_c=b_2\frac{\bar P_z}{\bar Q},\quad E_Y=b_3\frac{\bar Y}{\bar Q} \]

Linear model only. The slope is not itself a percent elasticity.

Simple line. \(\hat Y=49.667-2.1576x\). Both p = 0.000. Slope: −2.16 units per extra dollar. Ignore the intercept 49.67. Means 37.8 and 5.5. Elasticity −0.31, so a 1% price rise cuts quantity 0.31%. R² = 0.9757.

Multiple. \(\hat Q=310.245-12.203\,\text{Price}+0.008\,\text{Ads}+2.677\,\text{GPP}\). Expected signs: −, +, +. Only price is significant (p = 0.037): about −12,200 units per extra dollar. Ads p = 0.971 and GPP p = 0.429 → elasticities 0. Means: Q 175, P 15.1, ads 125, GPP 17.95. Price elasticity \(= -12.202\times 15.1/175 \approx -1.05\) (slightly elastic). The slide prints −1.503 next to that product; recompute it. R² = 0.7896. Prob > F = 0.0187.

Ch 3–4 · planned behavior

Demand = willingness and ability, several prices, a stated time period. \(u\) is unobserved.

Purchase actually happens → economics, demand theory. Intention only → psychology, TPB.

\[ \mathrm{intent}=\beta_0+\beta_1\mathrm{att}+\beta_2\mathrm{sn}+\beta_3\mathrm{pbc}+\varepsilon_1 \] \[ \mathrm{beh}=\delta_0+\delta_1\mathrm{intent}+\delta_2\mathrm{pbc}+\varepsilon_2 \]

All five slopes are hypothesized positive. Ajzen (1991): attitude, subjective norm, and PBC raise intention; intention and PBC are hypothesized to raise behavior.

Attitude: favorable or unfavorable evaluation (happy, wise, fashion, attractive, beneficial). Subjective norm: social pressure (family, friends, someone respected or the boss, experts, neighbors). PBC: difficulty, experience, obstacles (money, autonomy, calm, knowledge). Intention: will, intend, expect. Behavior: have visited, driven, compared loans, paid a deposit, installed a charger.

Likert: 5 strongly agree down to 1 strongly disagree. Row means. intent, not int, because int is Stata’s integer keyword. att and sn over 5 items, pbc over 7, intent over 5, beh over 5.

Bangkok, convenience sample, N = 550, file EV.xlsx. Gender: 1 male, 2 female, 3 LGBTQ+.

Ch 5 · CVM and PSM

Reservation price = height of the demand curve. 1st unit up to 20 baht; 10th unit up to 9 baht. Methods: CVM and Van Westendorp PSM.

Double bound: \(P_0=60\), \(P_L=40\), \(P_H=80\). Yes at 60 → ask 80. No at 60 → ask 40.

[yes, yes] WTP ≥ 80, premium, skim. [yes, no] 60 ≤ WTP < 80, value, keep the base price. [no, yes] 40 ≤ WTP < 60, discounts or a cheaper version. [no, no] WTP < 40, exclude from communications.

Buyers: 25% at 80, 50% at 60, 80% at 40. Out of 10,000: 2,500, 5,000, 8,000.

PSM buy questions: expensive, cheap. Not-buy questions: too expensive, too cheap. Cumulative curves rise. Inverse curves (cheap, too cheap) fall.

PMC = expensive ∩ too cheap ≈ 68, lower bound. PME = too expensive ∩ cheap ≈ 92, upper bound. Acceptable range (68, 92). Below PMC, quality suspicion wins. Above PME, “too expensive” wins.

IDP = cheap ∩ expensive ≈ 83, the normal market price. OPP minimizes resistance. Egg graph: too-cheap and too-expensive meet near 0% around 70–80, so OPP is in that band. Drop a row unless too cheap < cheap < expensive < too expensive. File High Protein Egg.xlsx, do-file PSM_4lines.do, 100 respondents.

Ch 5 · NMS demand and revenue

Two extra questions: likelihood of buying at the person’s cheap price, and at their expensive price.

Grid: 42 rounds down to 40, 158 rounds up to 160. \((160-40)/12=10\). Prices 40, 50, …, 160.

Weights: 5 → 70%, 4 → 30%, 1–3 → 0. If the test price is between that person’s cheap and expensive prices, average the two weights. Otherwise 0. So 15% or 50% are the two worked non-zero cases at 80 baht.

Sample averages: 29.65% at 80 (about 30% of the market), 17.85% at 90, 9.75% at 100. Prices around 40–70 baht raise quality doubts and can lower purchase probability.

\[ \text{Revenue index} = \text{Price}\times\text{purchase probability} \]

80 × 30% = 24. 85 × 25% = 21.25. 90 × 20% = 18.

Revenue-maximizing price on the egg chart: 70 baht. Target population 72,000. Total revenue illustration: \(2{,}450\times 72{,}000=176{,}400{,}000\) baht. Do-file: “NMS demand curve and revenue.”

Thirty-second pass before you go in

Sign first. Own price negative. Cross-price: + substitutes, − complements. Income: + normal, − inferior.
Then compare with 1. Size above 1 elastic, luxury if it is an income elasticity and positive. Size below 1 inelastic, or a necessity if income elasticity is positive.
Revenue follows the bigger percent change. If quantity moves more than price, revenue follows quantity. If price moves more, revenue follows price.
p against 0.10, 0.05, 0.01. Insignificant coefficient: “no effect,” and elasticity 0. Do not multiply.
TPB path. att, sn, pbc → intention, all significant, attitude largest. intention → behavior. Direct pbc → behavior is not significant (0.177).
Chapter 5 path. CVM pairs, then PSM crossings, then 70 / 30 / 0 weights, then price × probability. Launch between 68 and 92. Price the egg cake at 70 for revenue.
