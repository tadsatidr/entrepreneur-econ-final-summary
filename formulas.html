Ch 1 · Demand & elasticity

Demand = willingness and ability to buy, at various prices, over a period. Law of demand: P↑ → Qd↓.

Movement along D: own price only. Shift right: income ↑ (normal good), preference ↑, substitute price ↑, complement price ↓. Blue jeans at $60: 600 → 900.

Market = sum of individual Q at one price: $12 → 4 + 5 + 100 = 109; $11 → 5 + 6 + 130 = 141.

\[E=\frac{\%\Delta Q}{\%\Delta P}=\frac{\Delta Q}{\Delta P}\times\frac{\bar P}{\bar Q},\quad \bar P=\tfrac{P_1+P_2}{2},\ \bar Q=\tfrac{Q_1+Q_2}{2}\]

Slide example: (10, 100) → (12, 50): ΔP = 2, ΔQ = −50, P̄ = 11, Q̄ = 75, 18.2% and −66.7% → 3.67, elastic. Shortcut −25 × 11/75 = −11/3.

E = −2: price −1% → sales +2%.

|E| > 1 elastic · < 1 inelastic · = 1 unit · ∞ perfectly elastic (horizontal) · 0 perfectly inelastic (vertical).

TR: elastic → TR moves opposite to P; inelastic → TR moves with P; unit → unchanged. Linear D: TR 350, 600, 750, 800 (max, unit elastic, Q 40, P 20), 750, 600, 350, 0.

Cross \(E_c\): + substitutes, − complements (meat & dairy −0.21). Income \(E_y\): + normal (> 1 luxury, < 1 necessity), − inferior.

Ch 2 · Regression & Stata

\[Q^d_x=\beta_0+\beta_1P_x+\beta_2P_z+\beta_3Y+u\]

β = true (unknown) parameter; b = sample estimate. \(H_0:\beta=0\) vs \(H_1:\beta\neq0\). Reject if p < α; α = 0.01, 0.05, 0.10.

Stars: p < 0.01 *** · < 0.05 ** · < 0.10 * · otherwise none. 0.000 → ***, 0.037 → **, 0.095 → *, 0.57 → none.

Significant: “+1 in X → Q changes by b units, other factors constant.” Insignificant: “no significant effect”, elasticity = 0.

\[E_d=b_1\frac{\bar P_x}{\bar Q},\ E_c=b_2\frac{\bar P_z}{\bar Q},\ E_y=b_3\frac{\bar Y}{\bar Q}\]

Stata columns: Coefficient (sign + size), Std. err., t = coef/SE, P>|t| = p-value, 95% CI (contains 0 → insignificant at 5%), _cons = intercept, R² = SS Model / SS Total, Prob > F = whole-model test.

Ex 1 regress y x: ŷ = 49.667 − 2.1576x, both p = 0.000, R² 0.9757, N 10. Price +$1 → Q −2.16 units. Ignore intercept (no economic meaning). sum y x: 37.8, 5.5 → E = −0.31 (inelastic). Command window can’t save; do-file can. Plot: twoway (scatter y x) (lfit y x).

Ex 2 reg qd price advertise gpp: expected signs −, +, +. Price −12.203 (p 0.037**) → −12,200 units per $1 (Q in thousands). Ads 0.008 (p 0.971), GPP 2.677 (p 0.429) → unchanged, elasticity 0. Means 175, 15.1, 125, 17.95. R² 0.7896, Prob > F 0.0187. Price elasticity: slide prints −1.503; −12.202 × 15.1/175 ≈ −1.05 → elastic either way.

Ch 3 · TPB

Qx1 purchase occurred (intention = purchase) → economics / demand theory. Qx2 intention only (≠ purchase) → psychology / TPB. u = unobserved.

\[\text{intent}=\beta_0+\beta_1\text{att}+\beta_2\text{sn}+\beta_3\text{pbc}+\varepsilon_1\] \[\text{beh}=\delta_0+\delta_1\text{intent}+\delta_2\text{pbc}+\varepsilon_2\]

All five hypothesized + (Ajzen 1991).

Attitude: favorable/unfavorable evaluation (happy, wise, fashion, attractive, beneficial). SN: perceived social pressure (family, friends, someone respected/boss, experts/influencers, neighbors). PBC: difficulty in implementation, past experience, expected obstacles (money, autonomy, stress-free, own decision, technical knowledge, budget). Intention: motivation, effort (will consider, intend, encourage, willing to test-drive, expect in 3–5 yrs). Behavior: observable actions — preparatory (showroom, test drive, loan comparison) → ultimate (deposit, purchase, wall charger).

Likert 5 = strongly agree … 1 = strongly disagree.

Ch 5 · WTP, CVM, PSM

WTP = reservation price = height of demand curve (1st unit 20, 10th unit 9). Methods: CVM, Van Westendorp PSM.

Double-bounded CVM: P0 60 → yes → PH 80; no → PL 40.

[yes,yes] WTP ≥ 80 premium/loyal → skimming · [yes,no] 60 ≤ WTP < 80 value-for-money → keep base price · [no,yes] 40 ≤ WTP < 60 price-sensitive → discounts, codes, economy version · [no,no] < 40 non-consumers → exclude.

Buy shares 25% at 80, 50% at 60, 80% at 40; × 10,000 → 2,500 / 5,000 / 8,000.

PSM: too expensive (NOT BUY), expensive (BUY), cheap (BUY), too cheap (NOT BUY). Rising (cumulative): too expensive, expensive. Falling (inverse): cheap, too cheap.

PMC = expensive ∩ too cheap ≈ 68 (lower) · PME = too expensive ∩ cheap ≈ 92 (upper) · range 68–92 · OPP = too cheap ∩ too expensive, min resistance, 70–80 · IDP = cheap ∩ expensive, “normal/current market price”, ≈ 83.

Egg cake: 100 respondents; drop rows unless too cheap < cheap < expensive < too expensive; do-file PSM_4lines.do.

Ch 5 · NMS demand & revenue

NMS adds 2 questions: likelihood of buying at your cheap price and at your expensive price.

Grid: 42 → 40, 158 → 160, (160 − 40)/12 = 10 → 40, 50, …, 160.

Weights: 5 → 0.70, 4 → 0.30, 1–3 → 0. Price inside [cheap, expensive] → (W_cheap + W_expen)/2, else 0. Examples at 80: (30 + 0)/2 = 15%, (70 + 30)/2 = 50%.

Averages: 29.65% at 80 (≈ 30% of market), 17.85% at 90, 9.75% at 100. Peak 35.1% at 70. Below 70 → quality doubts (hormones, counterfeit, expiry) → lower probability.

\[\text{Revenue index}=\text{Price}\times\text{Probability}\]

80 × 30% = 24; 85 × 25% = 21.25; 90 × 20% = 18. Table max 70 × 35.1 = 2,457 → price at 70 baht.

Market 72,000 students: demand = 72,000 × probability; revenue ≈ 2,450 × 72,000 = 176,400,000 baht.
